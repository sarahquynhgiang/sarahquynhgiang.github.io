In contrast to typical definitions of drought, which describe this hazard as developing slowly, flash droughts are characterized by a rapid onset of drought conditions (e.g. lack of precipitation, heatwaves, soil moisture depletion) that often lead to outsized impacts. Since the first heavily-studied flash drought event in 2012, significant progress has been made in methods for identifying flash droughts: from the standardized precipitation index (SPI), soil moisture indices, evapotranspirative indices, to even remotely-sensed plant condition indices, identification methods vary widely in their observed variables. However, beyond a few benchmark flash drought events, little research exists that demonstrates just how impactful these events can be, particularly in sectors beyond agriculture.

We invite researchers and organizations from various backgrounds to join us for a 1.5-day in-person workshop discussing not only the current state of flash drought research through talks and posters, but also future potential directions through an interdisciplinary panel of stakeholders and researchers of different backgrounds who are also affected by drought.
